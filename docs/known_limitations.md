# Known limitations

Tengri is research code under active development (v0.1.0). The forward model
and inference pipeline work and are covered by 2000+ tests, but several
capabilities are partial. This page collects the caveats you are most
likely to hit.

## Verification status

No physics component has completed full external cross-validation
against FSPS, Prospector, or CIGALE. The reproduction notebooks under
`docs/reproduction/` compare specific components against established codes, but
the formal verification protocol (`docs/dev/verification-protocol.md`) lists
every major block as still in progress. **Do not treat fitted parameters as
publication-grade until your component has been cross-validated.**

## Approximations

Both precompute paths trade a bounded, quantified error for a large speedup,
and both earn it the same way: integrate everything that does not depend on a
free parameter once, at build time, and reduce each likelihood call to a small
contraction.

```{figure} _static/figures/precompute_schematic.png
:alt: An SSP grid under the GALEX and ugriz bandpasses, one band split into five equal-mass sub-bands, per-age-bin quadrature nodes, the stored tensor, the SFH age weights, and the composite-stellar-population sum.
:width: 100%

What `approx=WavePrecomp()` does to a real SSP grid, and where the star
formation history enters. **(a)** The grid holds one spectrum per age bin;
five are drawn, under the GALEX and ugriz bandpasses. SDSS *u* is followed
through the rest of the figure. **(b)** That band is cut into $K = 5$
sub-bands of *equal filter mass* — the five shaded areas are equal, so they
run narrow where the filter is most transmissive and wide out in the wings.
The partition depends on the filter alone. **(c)** Every age bin is then
integrated **separately** against that partition, and each gets its own $K$
nodes $\lambda^\star_{jk}$ (the tick rows): a node is that age bin's own
flux-weighted centroid within its sub-band, so it moves as the spectrum
ages. **(d)** What is stored is therefore a tensor, not a vector — $K$
numbers for every age bin, every metallicity and every band,
$\Phi_{ijbk}$. None of it depends on a free parameter, so it is all built
once. **(e)** At fit time the star formation history is integrated onto
that *same* age grid by the cloud-in-cell kernel, giving one mass weight
$w_j$ per age bin. **(f)** The composite-stellar-population flux is then
the double sum $f_b = \sum_j w_j \sum_k \Phi_{jk} A(\lambda^\star_{jk})$ —
over all 93 age bins and their 5 sub-bands, with the dust screen evaluated
only at the nodes. No wavelength grid is touched at any point in the
likelihood, and for this band the result lands within a few thousandths of
a percent of the exact integral. Generated by
`scripts/render_precompute_schematic.py`.
```

The reason a factorization like this needs care at all: the photometry
integral wants $\langle F_\nu A \rangle_b$ — the SSP spectrum times the dust
screen, averaged over the bandpass. Precomputing the two separately would
give $\langle F_\nu \rangle_b \langle A \rangle_b$, and the difference
between them is exactly the in-band covariance $\mathrm{Cov}_b(F_\nu, A)$.
That covariance is the entire error term, and the sub-band quadrature is what
buys it back. Additive emitters — dust IR, AGN, radio, X-ray — need none of
this: each is a sum of rank-one terms, so it factors through the filter
exactly and carries no quadrature error at all.

```{figure} _static/figures/precompute_subband_error.png
:alt: The GALEX FUV bandpass split into five equal-mass sub-bands with one quadrature node each, and the resulting convergence with K.
:width: 100%

Where the residual error lives. **(a)** The real GALEX FUV bandpass split
into $K = 5$ sub-bands of equal filter mass — the five shaded regions have
equal area under $T_b w$. Each carries one node $\lambda^\star$, the
template's own flux-weighted centroid there, so the screen is evaluated at
five points and held constant across each sub-band; the shaded gap between
the true screen and that step function is the whole of the approximation.
Because each node is a first moment, the first moment of the in-band
residual vanishes identically — a first-order correction on top of the
sub-band rule would add exactly zero. Edges and nodes are drawn from the
shipped `subband_quadrature` partition. Panel (a) is a mild configuration
($z = 0$, $\tau_\mathrm{diff} = 0.7$) where the $K = 0$ Taylor extrapolation
still nearly coincides with the true screen; it diverges as redshift and
optical depth grow, which is what the $K = 0$ points in **(b)** quantify.
**(b)** Worst-band error against the exact path over $z \le 1$ and
$\tau \le 2$. Generated by `scripts/render_precompute_schematic.py`.
```

- **`WavePrecomp` accelerates the photometry channel.** It precomputes the
  SSP × filter integrals, with free redshift via interpolation. Each filter is
  split into `n_subbands` sub-bands of equal filter mass (default 5), and the
  multiplicative screens — dust attenuation and IGM transmission — are
  **evaluated** at each sub-band's quadrature node, the template's own
  flux-weighted centroid there. Measured against the exact path across
  GALEX→WISE at z ≤ 1.5 with τ_diff = 0.7 / τ_bc = 1.0, the worst band agrees to
  ≲0.5%, and the optical/NIR bands to ≲0.01%. Accuracy improves as 1/K²; raise
  `WavePrecomp(n_subbands=8)` for the deep rest-UV. Emission that the quadrature
  cannot reach — nebular lines, AGN, dust IR — is still projected at the filter's
  effective wavelength.
- **`WavePrecomp(n_subbands=0)` restores the old first-order Taylor projection**
  which extrapolates the screen from each filter's effective wavelength instead
  of evaluating it. That linear-in-λ model is accurate for smooth attenuation
  curves (optical/IR) but biases rest-UV bands where the curve is steep: at
  z ≈ 2 with τ ≈ 0.5, SDSS *u/g* run 5–10% high and GALEX FUV >10× high. A
  build-time `UserWarning` flags such configurations. It is retained only for
  reproducing results from earlier versions.
- **`SpectrumPrecomp` accelerates the spectroscopy channel.** It precomputes a
  per-pixel effective-wavelength continuum LUT. It agrees with the exact path
  to machine precision with zero dust. With dust, it carries the same error
  class as `WavePrecomp` but far milder (pixel-wide vs filter-wide): typically
  ≲1% on the continuum, growing to ~10–15% only in the deep rest-UV at high
  optical depth. High spectral resolution auto-falls-back to the exact
  wave-grid path. **Component coverage:** stellar, dust IR, radio, X-ray, AGN,
  and non-baked nebular (Cue/CloudyGrid) are included in the spectrum LUT.
  Nebular emission is reddened by the young-limit screen, matching the exact
  path. BakedIn nebular is carried by the stellar LUT.
- **Joint photometry + spectroscopy is supported under precompute.** Either
  precompute opt-in builds both LUT families; the forward pass projects both
  channels in one fused, cached JIT kernel. For independent per-channel tuning,
  pass `approx=(WavePrecomp(n_z=200), SpectrumPrecomp())`.
  Velocity dispersion / LSF are not applied on the per-pixel continuum LUT
  (`SpectrumPrecomp`'s low-to-medium-R domain). Use `approx=None` for the exact
  LSF-convolved spectrum.

## Inference backends

Each backend is tagged `primary` or `experimental`. Experimental backends
carry explicit `[POOR MIXING]` or `[UNSTABLE]` flags (e.g. `mcmc_ghmc`,
`mcmc_mclmc`, `pathfinder`, `native_vi_*`). Use `primary` backends for science.
See [Choosing an inference method](method_selection).

## Memory

NUTS/VI warmup peaks far above the resident forward model — 20+ GB on
`dense_basis` D≈8 with `dense_mass_matrix=True`. Rules:

- Run **one** NUTS/VI fit per Python process.
- On D ≥ 8, use `dense_mass_matrix=False` or `mcmc_hmc`.
- `WavePrecomp` keeps photometry fits in the few-GB range.

Full detail: `docs/dev/notebook_orchestration_oom.md`.

## Physics caveats

- **AGN torus:** `agn/torus.py` toy models are not for science. Use
  `agn_model="skirtor"` (Stalevski et al. 2016).
- **Dust IR emission templates** must be loaded from `data/`; analytic
  fallbacks are not suitable for science.
- **Dust emission JIT scaling:** DL07-family dust-emission models (DL07, DL14, and tabulated variants) build very large JIT graphs when `dust_umin` is left free in the sampler. The compiled graph can exceed 2 GB, causing slowdowns (~150× slower) in nested sampling backends. Workarounds: (1) fix `dust_umin` rather than fitting it, or (2) use fixed-redshift models where the precomputation (`approx=WavePrecomp()`) amortizes the graph across many calls. Technical details are tracked in the repository's engineering notes ([docs/dev/known_bugs.md on GitHub](https://github.com/suchethac/tengri/blob/main/docs/dev/known_bugs.md)).
- **Nebular backends:** `Cue` and `CloudyGrid` require bare-stellar SSP grids;
  `BakedIn` cannot vary `neb_logU` / `neb_fesc`. Four of six `tengri.recipes`
  need bare-stellar grids.
- **Metallicity** is `log10(Z)` absolute on the SSP grid; user-facing
  `neb_logZ_gas` is `log10(Z/Z_sun)`.
