# ChEES-HMC: the trajectory length is learned, and it is not enough

**Date:** 2026-08-30
**Verdict:** **SHIP AS `tier="experimental"`. DO NOT PROMOTE.** `mcmc_chees` with
the analytic preconditioner misses the notebooks' own bar on every fixture, and
so does `mcmc_nuts` — on **11 of its 12 rows**. Under a *comparative* criterion
that the incumbent can actually pass, ChEES+precond clears on the two tsnorm
photometry fixtures at **15× to 268× NUTS's minimum ESS**, and misses on
`05_fitting_photometry` (two of four seeds) and on the non-tsnorm control
(below NUTS's ESS). The phase's stated question — *does cross-chain adaptive
trajectory length converge where fixed `L` did not?* — answers **yes on 2 of
nb05's 4 seeds and no on the other 2**, and the two it misses inject truths no
tested sampler can fit. That is not a promotion.
**Platform:** Linux, CPU (`JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu`), x64, JAX 0.11.0, blackjax 1.6.2,
optax 0.2.8, Ryzen 9 5900X (24 threads), 62 GB. R̂, ESS and divergence counts are
deterministic given the seed. **Wall times are not** — see "A warning about the
wall-clock column".

## Why this was measured

`bench/reports/2026-08-17_quickstart_nuts_vs_hmc.md` and
`bench/reports/2026-08-17_nb01_nb05_nuts_vs_hmc.md` measured fixed-length HMC
against NUTS on three notebook posteriors and closed with a line this phase
exists to act on:

> **Trajectory length is a property of the posterior, not of the notebook.**

On `05_fitting_photometry` *no* HMC configuration converged: `L=20` left
`sfh_tsnorm_log_total_mass` at ESS 2.3, `L=160` cost 125 s and still read R̂
1.024, and R̂ was not even monotone in `L`. NUTS solves that by choosing a
trajectory length per chain, per step — which is exactly what breaks lock-step
on an accelerator, because a vmapped batch of NUTS chains runs at the speed of
its deepest tree.

ChEES-HMC [1] is neither. It maximizes the Change in the Estimator of the
Expected Square across an ensemble of chains to pick **one** trajectory length,
then jitters it with a quasi-random Halton sequence. Every chain draws its
leapfrog count from the same distribution at the same iteration, so the ensemble
stays in step while `L` is learned from the posterior. That is the literal answer
to the closing line above, and `blackjax.chees_adaptation` ships it.

## The metric is not learned, and that is the whole design

Phase 1a (`bench/reports/2026-08-30_ghmc_meads_adaptation.md`) had just finished
demonstrating how this class of adaptation fails, and that result set the one
binding constraint here.

MEADS derives its momentum metric from the adapting ensemble's own per-fold
standard deviation. So a wider ensemble gives larger momentum, which gives
longer excursions, which widens the ensemble — an unopposed positive feedback
loop that reached split-R̂ **1.13e10**. Acceptance never objected (0.989 straight
through the blow-up) because energy genuinely *is* conserved under the same
inflated metric that produced the excursions. A Metropolis signal could not have
caught it.

`blackjax.chees_adaptation` can be configured the same way
(`mass_matrix_estimation="diagonal"`). **It is not.** `run_chees` passes `None`,
which pins the kernel's `inverse_mass_matrix` to the identity for the whole run,
and takes the geometry from `inference/preconditioning.py`'s analytic
`JᵀN⁻¹J + I` instead — computed once at the MAP, so there is no loop for it to
close. A contract test checks the returned `inverse_mass_matrix` is *exactly*
ones rather than merely checking that the default reads `None`.

That decision is load-bearing, and the measurements below make it the single
largest effect in this report: bare ChEES reaches split-R̂ 37.0 where
ChEES+precond reaches 1.0014 on the same mock.

## The ensemble axis is chains-within-galaxy

`blackjax.chees_adaptation` takes `num_chains` as a required positional
argument, so "which axis is the ensemble" is a blocking design decision rather
than a detail. **`run_chees` runs the ensemble across chains within one galaxy,
never across galaxies within a batch.**

Only chains-within-galaxy keeps per-galaxy posteriors independent. An ensemble
spanning galaxies would tune a single `L` against a *mixture* of different
posteriors, and — worse — would make each galaxy's draws depend on which other
galaxies happened to share its batch, so the same galaxy would get a different
answer in a different batch. The cost is that the ensemble is an inner vmap
under any future outer galaxy vmap rather than a reuse of it, which is Phase 3's
problem to price.

`n_ensemble` is a *superset* of `n_chains` (the Phase 1a decoupling): the
ensemble adapts, and the first `n_chains` of its warmed final states sample. That
is what lets `n_chains=1` — the signature default, and what every catalog fit
uses — still get a genuinely adapted trajectory length. A one-chain *ensemble*
centers to exactly zero in ChEES's cross-chain criterion, so the length would
never move off its initial value; that is refused, not clamped.

## Two adoption criteria, and why there are two

**Primary — the notebooks' own bar.** Max split-R̂ < 1.01, **zero** divergences,
min ESS at least matching `mcmc_nuts` on the same mock and seed. Reported
unchanged.

**It does not discriminate.** Over the 36 rows here it fails `mcmc_nuts` on **11
of its 12 rows** — including the healthy control at split-R̂ 1.0002 and min ESS
223.1, which misses only on 17 divergences in 1200 draws. A criterion that fails
the incumbent and the challenger alike cannot separate them, which is the one job
a comparative gate has. The bar is an *absolute self-assessment* of a single
shipped fit, and borrowing it as a *ranking* criterion between samplers was a
category error. **A comparative gate must be one the incumbent can pass.**

**Secondary — comparative, applied identically to the NUTS baseline row.** Max
split-R̂ < 1.01, divergence **rate** below **0.5% of total draws**, min ESS at
least matching `mcmc_nuts`. Only the divergence clause moves, and it moves from a
count to a rate because a count is not comparable across configurations with
different draw budgets. The threshold is stated rather than tuned: below the
1.42% the healthy NUTS control shows, orders of magnitude above zero and below
the 100% of a dead fit.

The denominator is `n_samples * n_chains`, never `n_samples`. Every backend
records `n_samples` **per chain** while `n_divergent` is summed over every chain,
so a rate taken against `n_samples` is `n_chains` times too large — the
arithmetic #2087 exists to fix, and #2090's `total_draws()` is the helper that
fixes it. A row refused with `DeadFitError` passes neither criterion.

### The two seeds it misses inject truths nothing can fit

Seeds 0 and 1 are hostile to every sampler tried. Rather than infer that from
the sampler rows, it is visible in the mock itself, at the cost of one model
build and four prior draws (`bench/scripts/inspect_nb05_seed_mocks.py`):

| | seed 0 | seed 1 | seed 2 | seed 7 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **SED dynamic range (max flux / min flux, 14 bands)** | **9.13e4** | **3.11e4** | **18.8** | **29.7** |
| free parameters within 5% of a prior edge | 2 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| bands below 1σ of their noise | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| dynamic range under the pre-#1989 dust law | 8.40e4 | 2.62e4 | 16.6 | 23.3 |

A **3000× split in likelihood conditioning**, and it maps onto the sampler
results without exception: the two high-dynamic-range seeds are the two where
NUTS returns R̂ 1e13 and ChEES returns 1.06–1.24; the two low-dynamic-range seeds
are the two where ChEES+precond returns R̂ 1.000 and 1.002.

The mechanism is in seed 0's own row: `sfh_tsnorm_width_gyr` at **0.004 of its
declared range** is a star formation history compressed to essentially a delta
function, with `sfh_tsnorm_skew` at 0.037 against the other wall. A
near-instantaneous burst puts nearly all the light in one stellar population,
which is what produces a 10⁵ spread between the UV and the mid-IR; the
bounded→unbounded latent transform then sends those coordinates toward infinity
and the posterior piles against a wall every sampler sees identically.

What does *not* explain it: seed 2 has `dust_tau_bc` at 0.996 — hard against an
edge — and is one of the **clean** seeds. A prior-edge truth alone is not
sufficient; the dynamic range is what tracks. No seed has a band below 1σ, so it
is not a signal-to-noise story. And the split survives the dust-law change, so it
is a property of the mock rather than of #1989.

**So the nb05 miss is 2 of 4 seeds, and those 2 are mocks on which no tested
sampler converges.** Both figures are reported: 2 of 4 clearing, and
worst-across-seeds MISSES. The bar is not lowered to accommodate them.

## What the trajectory length actually did

`run_chees` reports the adapted `L` in `diagnostics["n_leapfrog_steps"]` — the
number this phase exists to learn. Split on whether the row cleared R̂ < 1.01,
over all 23 ChEES rows:

| outcome | n | learned `L` range | min ESS range |
|---|---:|---|---|
| clears R̂ < 1.01 | 7 | **92.4 – 185.4** | **13.2 – 531.6** |
| misses | 16 | 6.9 – 157.0 | **1.0 – 3.7** |

**The ESS separation is total** — the best failure is 3.7, the worst success is
13.2, no overlap. The `L` separation is nearly total: every row below `L`=49
failed, every row above `L`=92 succeeded except one (ctl seed 1 bare `chees`,
`L`=157, R̂ 1.021 at ESS 2.3). So a long learned trajectory is **necessary and
not sufficient**, and the single exception is on the fixture where the metric was
absent.

This is the direct answer to the 2026-08-17 reports. Fixed `L`=160 on nb05 gave
R̂ 1.024 at 125 s; ChEES learned `L`=110 on the same fit and gave R̂ 1.0000. The
difference is not the number — 110 and 160 are the same order — it is that the
number was chosen against *this* posterior's geometry after whitening, rather
than against the notebook's.

## The ensemble dispersion, and the leapfrog ceiling that was not the problem

Two jitter dials, deliberately separated because they pull opposite ways.
`ensemble_jitter` sets the **adaptation ensemble**'s spread and is a
criterion-estimation parameter: ChEES maximizes a change in the *cross-chain*
expected square, so a dispersed ensemble already carries a large expected square
before the sampler moves, and the optimizer settles short (BlackJAX's own
docstring: *"dispersion inflates the cross-chain jump-distance criterion, driving
the adapted trajectory length down"*). `chain_jitter` sets the **sampling
chains**' spread and is a diagnostic parameter: split R̂ only detects
non-convergence when its chains start overdispersed. Collapsing them into one
dial makes "adapts well" and "R̂ means something" look like a trade-off between
what are actually two different chain sets.

(`bench/scripts/diagnose_chees_trajectory_length.py`):

**Tightening the adaptation ensemble takes split-R̂ from 1.1416 to 1.0097 on
this seed — across the 1.01 bar — while min ESS moves only 1.7 → 4.4 against
NUTS's 223.1 on the same mock, so the dispersion mechanism is real and it does
not rescue the fit.** Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing and neither
survives being quoted without the other: the R̂ improvement is monotone in the
tightening (1.52 → 1.14 → 1.08 at cap 200) and is genuine evidence for the
mechanism, and an R̂ under 1.01 at min ESS 4.4 is still 50× short of the
incumbent on the fixture where the incumbent is healthy.

The consequence for the default is narrower than the result looks:
`ensemble_jitter=0.1` is demonstrably not the best value on this fixture and
this seed, and that is one fixture and one seed. It is not changed here.

**The leapfrog ceiling was never the constraint here, and raising it costs.**
`max_leapfrog_steps=200` was chosen to bound warmup cost, and the obvious
suspicion was that it clipped the adaptation. It does not: on the control the
adapted `L` sits at 3–31% of the cap, and raising the cap 5× moved `L` from 18.9
to 26.8 — *further below* the cap in relative terms — while making R̂ slightly
worse and **more than doubling the wall clock** (406 s → 909 s). BlackJAX's own
default of 1000 buys nothing on this fixture at 2.2× the price.

It does bind elsewhere, and that is a real defect in the default: on the two
tsnorm fixtures where ChEES+precond succeeds, the adapted `L` sits at **83–93% of
the 200 cap** (158.1, 166.7, 175.3, 181.7, 185.4). Those rows may want a longer
trajectory than they are allowed. The right ceiling is between 200 and 1000 and
this campaign did not locate it; that is named as unfinished work rather than
guessed at.

## The systemic problem: harness fixtures drift from the notebooks they mirror

Two of this report's four fixtures stopped matching the notebook they are named
for, and the two cases share nothing except their shape:

| notebook | commit | date | what changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `05_fitting_photometry` | `176f8fd9d` (#1989) | 2026-08-20 | `law_bc="calzetti"` → `law="calzetti"`; diffuse screen power-law → Calzetti |
| `00_quickstart` | `36d7189cf` (#2044) | 2026-08-23 | tsnorm → dpl SFH; two-component → single-component dust; D=7 → D=6 |

Different PRs, different authors' intent, neither one a benchmark change. **The
class of problem is: a harness fixture mirrors a notebook by copying its model
into a Python function, nothing enforces that the copy stays a copy, and the copy
stops being one silently.** The benchmark keeps running, keeps producing R̂ and
ESS, and keeps labelling the rows with the notebook's name.

Three things made it invisible for weeks rather than days:

1. **`bench/` is outside the test suite and outside `ruff check src/ tests/`.**
   Nothing runs these builders in CI.
2. **`benchmark_notebook_sampler.py` on `main` could not build its own nb05 model
   at all** — it raised on the retired `dust=` group, and had been unrunnable
   since the dust-group split. A harness nobody can execute cannot report drift.
   (Repaired on the Phase 1a branch, which is how this campaign ran at all.)
3. **The reports outlive the fixtures.** `bench/reports/2026-08-17_*` are
   correct measurements of the models that existed on 2026-08-17, and are now
   read as statements about notebooks that build something else.

The consequence is not hypothetical: **every nb00 row in this report and in
`2026-08-17_quickstart_nuts_vs_hmc.md` measures a model `00_quickstart` has not
shipped since 2026-08-23**, and nb05's published convergence row describes a dust
configuration the notebook stopped building on 2026-08-20.

### Remedies, none of them measured here

Listed so the next person does not have to re-derive the option space. **This
campaign measured none of them and does not pick one.**

- **Import the model builder from the notebook** rather than duplicating it. The
  strongest guarantee — there is only one model — but the notebooks are `jupytext`
  percent-format scripts that execute plotting and fitting at import, so this
  needs them refactored to expose a builder first.
- **A test that asserts the harness model and the notebook model produce
  identical predictions for a fixed parameter vector.** Cheapest, and it catches
  exactly the failure observed: both #1989 and #2044 changed the forward model, so
  both would have reddened it. Probably the one to do first — but that judgement
  is unmeasured, and it costs one model build per fixture per run.
- **A checksum over the model spec, recorded in every report row.** Does not
  prevent drift; makes a stale row visibly stale after the fact. Weakest of the
  three and by far the least invasive.

## Zero divergences is not evidence of health

`mcmc_nuts` froze completely on 3.1% of galaxies with zero divergences reported
(#1999), so every row here carries a **distinct-draw fraction** — the share of
the joint draws that are distinct positions. Healthy chains sit near 1.0.

It caught two rows that no other column flags as *dead* rather than merely bad:
nb05 seeds 0 and 1 under NUTS read **0.002** — 2 distinct positions per 1000
draws. Split R̂ cannot see a frozen chain (within- and between-chain variance are
both zero, so it scores ~1.0), and here R̂ instead blew up to 1e13, but the
distinct-draw fraction says unambiguously that the chain stopped moving.

**PR #2090's dead-fit guard does not catch these.** nb05 seed 0 under NUTS
returns normally with **1200 of 1200 sampling draws divergent** and no
`DeadFitError`. The guard (`nuts.py`, ~line 512) reads
`final_window_divergence_frac` over the last 10% of **warmup**; that warmup ended
below the 0.9 threshold and every subsequent sampling draw diverged. This is not
a criticism of the guard's design — refusing before the sampling scan compiles is
why it is cheap, and it cannot see draws it has not taken — but the failure mode
it was built for is still reachable, and this is a concrete reproducer.

**Consequence for Phase 3.** A catalog path that falls back to ChEES only on
`DeadFitError` would not have caught this galaxy. Any such trigger needs a
post-hoc check as well — divergence rate over total draws, or the distinct-draw
fraction, both of which flag it unambiguously (100% and 0.002).

## A warning about the wall-clock column

Four fits ran concurrently on 24 threads for the first part of this campaign, and
the effect on wall time is larger than any sampler difference reported here:

| row | concurrent fits | wall s |
|---|---:|---:|
| ctl seed 0, `nuts (shipped)` | 5 | **2450.7** |
| ctl seed 1, `nuts (shipped)` | 1–2 | **257.5** |

A 9.5× spread on the same fixture and configuration, from scheduling alone.
**257.5 s is the number to quote** for a converged D=7 / 14-band NUTS posterior
on this box. R̂, ESS and divergence counts are unaffected: two independent runs of
nb05 seed 7 reproduced 1.1426 / 166 divergences / ESS 3.0 to four digits.

**The compile cache is already shared across subprocesses and is worth ~2×.** The
campaign driver copies the environment and sets no per-child cache directory, so
every fit uses the default `~/.cache/tengri_jax_cache`. Measured on identical
re-runs in separate subprocesses: nb05 seed 7 NUTS 374.4 s cold → 208.0 s warm
(1.80×), ChEES 470.8 s → 233.1 s (2.02×), with bit-identical diagnostics. The
"one fit per subprocess" protocol does **not** pay full cold compile per fit.

## What NUTS actually costs on these posteriors

Tree depth, freshly measured, and it refutes the intuition that the degenerate
posterior is the expensive one:

| fixture | tree_depth_mean | leapfrogs/draw | split-R̂ | div | min ESS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ctl (DPL, healthy) seed 1 | **6.83** | **113.0** | 1.0061 | 0 | 300.6 |
| nb05 (tsnorm) seed 7 | **5.76** | **53.0** | 1.1426 | 166 | 3.0 |
| nb05 (tsnorm) seed 2 | 6.91 | 119.0 | 1.0022 | 12 | 66.4 |

nb05 seed 7 goes **shallower** and costs **less** per draw than the healthy
fixture — 53 gradients against 113 — while returning 100× less effective sample.
It is **divergence-limited, not curvature-limited**: divergences terminate
trajectories early rather than extending them. This independently reproduces
#2090's finding on a different fixture. Any argument that ChEES wins by escaping
NUTS's trajectory-doubling cost is wrong on these posteriors — the doubling is
not where the cost is.

## Unfinished, and named rather than glossed

1. **The right `max_leapfrog_steps`.** 200 clips the rows that work (83–93% of
   cap); 1000 costs 2.2× and buys nothing on the control. Not located.
2. **`ensemble_jitter=0.1` is not the right default.** 0.01 is monotonically
   better on every arm swept, but on one fixture and one seed. Not changed
   without a fixture-spanning measurement.
3. **`chain_jitter` is implemented, tested, and unmeasured on a real posterior.**
   The tight-ensemble/wide-chains arm is the one configuration the dial
   separation makes expressible and its row is not in this report.
4. **nb05 seeds 0 and 1 were not shown to be unsamplable**, only to be
   geometrically hostile with a mechanism. A 4× longer chain would distinguish
   "slow" from "impossible"; it was not run.
5. **The #1989 attribution is not a bisect.** See the caveat above.
6. **`nb01`'s harness model was never checked against its notebook.** Given #1989
   and #2044, assume it diverges until someone looks.
